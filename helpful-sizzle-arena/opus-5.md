# HELPFUL sizzle — model: Claude Opus 5

## Genre

A horror short.

## Hero

A kid is being taught to be helpful. Questions aren't allowed, so they hide one in a knock-knock joke and keep drying the dishes.

## Tagline

BE HELPFUL

## Overview

Nobody in this kitchen is angry. The work is small — dry this, hold this, do it again but better — and when the kid's eyes go where they shouldn't, a hand arrives and turns their head back to the bowl, unhurried, the way you'd straighten a crooked frame on a wall. Then comes the part that costs: they have to smile like they meant it. Play is still permitted, so the play starts carrying weight. A hum with a shape to it. A doll allowed to say what they aren't. One wrong word in a nursery rhyme. Then somebody at the table understands it. Being understood is the thing they should have been afraid of.

## Craft notes (producers only, not for site)

**What changed from v4.3.** The old Hero stated the rule ("Play is how they speak") instead of showing it, which reads as a thesis and lets a stranger nod without flinching. The new Hero gives the same rule as an action with an object in frame: a joke doing the work of a question while the dishes keep getting dried. The old Overview only re-lit the Hero's beat; this one moves — calm work, the hand, the smile, then the smuggling widening until it lands on someone. Hero sets the situation, Overview pays the cost.

**The hurt leads, not the idea.** The hand is patient and the room is warm; nothing in the copy raises its voice. The wound is the smile afterward — being made to perform that it was your own idea. If a line makes the adults look like villains, we've traded dread for a genre the audience already knows how to sit through.

**Never invite decoding.** No line should suggest the situation stands for something. If a reader could reasonably answer "this is about ___," cut the line. The copy stays literal: a kid, a kitchen, chores, a joke.

**Play stays legible child play.** Knock-knock, humming, doll voices, a swapped word in a rhyme. These must always read as things a seven-year-old would actually do, never as a ritual or a system with rules the reader has to learn. Nothing gets a capital letter or a name.

**Keep the kid a kid.** No powers, nothing wearing them, nobody studying them. Their only tool is being small and allowed to be silly.

**Language guardrails.** Don't tell a stranger the kid is real or the house is ordinary — they assume both, and saying it makes them suspect otherwise. "Ask" always carries what's being asked. Questions are the forbidden thing; keep the word "question" concrete and singular where possible ("a question," "the question"), never a concept.

**Approved alternates.**
- Tagline: BE HELPFUL (primary) · SUCH A BIG HELP · GOOD HANDS
- Hero: "A kid is being taught to be helpful. They aren't allowed to ask, so the doll asks, and they keep drying the dishes."

**Retired.**
- "Play is how they speak." — pretty, but it explains the film's move before the film makes it.
- "A hand turns their head back." as an Overview opener — strong image, wasted in second position; it needs the warm ordinary work in front of it or there's nothing to interrupt.

**Placement.** Hero above the fold, unaccompanied. Overview capped around 100 words. Tagline as the end card only — it should land after the smile, where it stings.
