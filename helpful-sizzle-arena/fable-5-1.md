# HELPFUL sizzle — model: Claude Fable 5.1

## Genre

A horror short.

## Hero

A kid is learning to be helpful. It's going well. They hardly look up anymore.

## Tagline

EYES ON YOUR WORK.

## Overview

Kitchen table, after dinner. Nobody is yelling. Nobody needs to. When the kid's eyes drift, a hand settles on the back of their head and steers it down, and they smile like looking down was their idea. Play is allowed. Questions aren't. So they play — and somewhere in the playing they start building a word they aren't allowed to say out loud, and wait to see who at the table notices.

## Craft notes (producers only, not for site)

**What changed from v4.3 and why**

- v4.3 Hero ("A kid has to be helpful. Play is how they speak.") stated the mechanism up front and left the Overview with nothing to do but restate the image. The new Hero withholds the mechanism and sells the situation as a success story ("It's going well"), so the third sentence lands as a bruise: a kid who has stopped looking up is a kid who has been trained. The irony does the hook's work without naming a second meaning.
- The hand moves from Hero to Overview. It is the film's single strongest image, and it reads harder after the stranger has been told everything is fine. "Steers it down" keeps it physical and against-the-will without escalating to violence the film doesn't show.
- "They smile like looking down was their idea" carries forward the v4.3 line ("smile like they meant it") but ties the smile to the specific act of complying, which is where the hurt sits.
- "Play is allowed. Questions aren't." is the doctrine's rule stated as a household rule. Four words, no capitalised concept, no invented vocabulary. The stranger already knows this kind of sentence from their own childhood.
- The final beat ("building a word they aren't allowed to say out loud") is the play-as-language idea from v4.3, now shown as an action with stakes rather than declared. We never say the word. We never say who is watching. "Who at the table notices" implies more than one adult and a live risk without dumping plot.
- Tagline: "BE HELPFUL" doubled the title. "EYES ON YOUR WORK." is the voice of the hand — an instruction the stranger has heard from a teacher or parent, now made sinister by context alone. It also rhymes with the Hero's "hardly look up." Keep the full stop; it should read as a correction, not a slogan.

**Guardrails checked**

- No "real kid," "ordinary house," or equivalent reassurance. The kitchen table, dinner, and the rule are shown, not labelled.
- No House / Correction / Cheat. No "the game."
- "Ask" appears only as "questions" with a clear object; there is no floating "ask."
- Kid stays a kid: no powers, no possession, no lab, no institution. The threat is an adult hand and a household rule.
- Hurt precedes idea in every unit: Hero (stopped looking up) before Overview's rule; the hand and the smile before the word.
- Overview does not restate Hero. Hero = the kid has adapted. Overview = what adapting costs and what they are still trying to do inside it.
- Nothing here explains a second meaning of "helpful." The title does that on its own once the stranger has read the Overview.

**Alternates, if the primaries test poorly**

- Hero alt: "A kid is learning to be helpful. When their head turns, a hand turns it back." (Stronger image, weaker irony; forces the Overview to give up the hand.)
- Tagline alt: "GOOD HELPERS DON'T ASK QUESTIONS." (More on-doctrine, but longer and closer to plot statement.)
- Tagline alt: "BE HELPFUL." (Retained from v4.3 for continuity with any printed material already in circulation.)

**Anti-references held**

No small-town ensemble, no artificial mind, no body-swap register. Keep every sentence at eye level with a child at a table. If a line would work on a Stranger Things or Get Out poster, cut it.
